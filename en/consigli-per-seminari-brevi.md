<p>Some tips for the "short" seminar at the graduation session:</p>

<ul>
  <li>The seminar should help the committee quickly understand what you worked on and what you achieved/implemented: prepare a very short description of the problem and clearly highlight what you built yourselves and what you instead reused</li>
  <li>Do not reuse the slides from the previous presentations you gave in the lab: the "short" presentation requires a different approach, precisely because of the time limits</li>
  <li>Remember to end with some conclusions, even very brief ones, that indicate where your work can provide other interesting ideas</li>
  <li>A 5-6 minute seminar does <strong>NOT</strong> mean taking a 20-minute seminar and speaking 4 times faster! The slides (number and content) must also be trimmed to the duration.</li>
  <li>It is perfectly acceptable to selectively decide to present only part of your work, for example, "<em>I developed three techniques to solve this problem: A, B, and C. For reasons of time, I will briefly illustrate only technique C.</em>"</li>
  <li>Make sure to look at the committee while you speak, and especially at your supervisor, who can give you cues (for example, if you are "running over" the planned time).</li>
  <li>Prepare a lot! Rehearse by enlisting friends and family as an audience. And time yourselves.</li>
  <li>During the short presentation you cannot improvise: if you get distracted and spend just 1 minute more on an introductory slide (only 60 seconds!) you might not manage to explain carefully what <strong>you</strong> built, because you may run out of time and have your presentation "cut off".</li>
  <li>Excessive animations during a short seminar make it impossible to read the content quickly.. so, in general, they are not recommended</li>
</ul>
