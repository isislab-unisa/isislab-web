  <h3>Information for Thesis Students</h3>
  <p>Internships and thesis work are carried out in the lab, because it is essential to have a constant relationship among the various members of ISISLab.</p>
  <p>Seminars presented by the lab members themselves are held regularly within ISISLab. The seminars have many purposes:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>getting to know your colleagues' work;</li>
    <li>gaining experience in presenting your own work;</li>
    <li>collectively evaluating your work and how it evolves;</li>
    <li>receiving suggestions and allowing the exchange of ideas.</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>Duties of an ISISLab Thesis Student</h3>
  <p>Being a thesis student in ISISLab means actively taking part in the lab's activities, currently held on the <a href="https://discord.gg/BTt5fUp" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer"><strong>Discord server</strong></a>, which include <strong>the seminars</strong> and all the other events promoted by the lab.</p>
  <p>The first step as a thesis student is to give your email address to your contact person, so that you are added to the lab's mailing list, which lets you receive all updates about events and activities, and to get your personal page on the site.</p>
  <p>You must provide <strong>weekly feedback</strong> to your contact person on the progress of your work and keep track of it on your personal page on this site, which will be assigned to you at the start of the internship.</p>
  <p>The page must contain at least the information listed below, but you can customize it as you like, following your personal style.</p>
  <ul>
    <li>Introduction (name, degree programme, interests).</li>
    <li>Thesis/internship topic, with the relevant supervisor/tutor.</li>
    <li>Seminars given (if any).</li>
    <li>Progress diary (to be divided into weeks/periods).</li>
  </ul>

  <h2>Seminar FAQ</h2>

  <h3>What Are the Seminars About?</h3>
  <p>About what you are working on, something you have read, or something fun/stimulating that has to do with computer science.</p>

  <h3>Who Can Attend the Seminars?</h3>
  <p>Of course, we have some fairly strict requirements: only human beings are admitted; so if you are a Klingon or a Unicorn, please send an email before attending 😉. Seriously, you do not need to be an ISISLab thesis student, or want to ask for a thesis in ISISLab, to attend the seminars. If you wish, you can also ask to join the lab's mailing list to be updated weekly about the seminars.</p>

  <h3>Will I Have to Give Seminars Too?</h3>
  <p>The answer (if you are an ISISLab thesis student) is "Of course!". Giving (several) seminars helps you learn to present your work in the most effective way and lets you improve your communication with others. It also serves as preparation for future job interviews: very often one of the standard questions is "What did you do in your thesis?", simply because it counts as one of the <em>free-choice questions</em>, which are very dangerous, since the best the candidate can offer is expected, given that they choose the topic themselves.</p>

  <h3>How Many Seminars Will I Have to Give?</h3>
  <p>Each thesis student usually gives 2-3 seminars, and each one has a different goal. The first seminar usually covers the technology/problem being addressed; the second begins to describe the solution being adopted, while the third (final) one describes the results obtained.</p>

  <h3>What Mistakes Should I Avoid?</h3>
  <p>Here is a list of the <a href="{{ 'en/errori-tipici-ai-seminari/' | relative_url }}">typical mistakes at seminars</a>, collected simply to spare you boring interruptions (from me!) every time you give one.</p>

  <h3>Is the Short Seminar at the Graduation Session Different from Those I Give in ISISLab?</h3>
  <p>Yes, of course! It obviously has a very different approach and requires extreme care to achieve its goal: presenting your work in very little time so that it is appreciated by the whole committee. See the <a href="{{ 'en/consigli-per-i-seminari-brevi/' | relative_url }}">tips on short seminars</a> that are available.</p>
