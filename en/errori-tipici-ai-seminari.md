<p>In no particular order… and without wanting to offend anyone, here are some of the typical mistakes I would like people to try to avoid.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">Design Mistakes</h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Mind the Length of the Seminar</h3>

<p>Typically a seminar should last about 45-60 minutes, to allow the audience to comment, ask questions, and start discussions, without the duration being too long. In general, it is not easy to set a limit on the "number" of slides... let's say that in 45 minutes, especially if the slides are very dense (code to explain, etc.), 25-30 is a reasonable number. Of course, if you have lots of diagrams/images/quick examples instead, this number can be increased, but not by too much. Consider, as a general measure, that you should speak for 45 minutes... if you get to 45 slides, it means <strong>on average</strong> one slide per minute... 60 seconds... let's say it's a barrage of slides!</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Design <strong><em>Your Own</em></strong> Seminar</h3>

<p>Believe us.. we all know that typing a search for your topic into your favourite search engine, with the file type "*.ppt", brings up ready-made seminars (surprised? 😉). And it is quite possible that one of us, before your seminar, runs such a search, just for the fun of seeing whether what you are about to present is really "your own work". So, it may be acceptable to take a nice animation, a fun example, some image, or even some idea… but the bulk of the seminar must be yours. One of the purposes of the seminar is to help you learn how to present your own work/project, in your own style, so take advantage of the opportunity and don't rely on Google 😉.</p>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">"Spoken" Mistakes</h2>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">"<em>This component</em> <strong>ritorna</strong> <em>the list …. </em>"</h3>

<p>AARGH! In Italian, "ritornare" is an intransitive verb: you <em>ritorni a casa</em> (return home), there is <em>Ritorno al futuro</em> (Back to the Future), etc… You must say <strong>restituisce una lista…</strong> ("returns a list"). Please!</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">"<em>My name is Rossi Mario</em>"</h3>

<p>AARGH again! Your name is "Mario Rossi"… you are not a robot, you are a person!</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">"<em>My name is Rossi Mario, student ID 05210/000001</em>"</h3>

<p>Even worse! We are not in the army..</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Work on Your English Pronunciation</h3>

<p>If you were born in Italy and perhaps in Campania, you will probably never manage an Oxford or Boston accent. That said, making blatant mispronunciations of technical terms is quite irritating. Here are some of the most frequent:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>engine</strong> is pronounced <em>engin</em> and NOT <em>engiain</em></li>
  <li><strong>header</strong> is pronounced <em>eder</em> and NOT <em>ider</em> (it comes from <em>head</em>)</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">"Written" Mistakes</h2>

<p>We are referring to the slides, of course.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Don't Use Dark Backgrounds</h3>

<p>They are hard to read, and make the whole seminar painful for the eyes.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Take Care of What You Have Written</h3>

<p>Please, try to carefully reread what you have written on the slides. Nothing irritates more than typos, or (worse!) blatant errors in Italian or English. In some seminars, sometimes even in thesis presentations, you find trivial typos, wrong capitalization, etc. Have someone else reread your slides, to catch those errors that seem invisible to you (who wrote them) (it's quite normal, you are certainly focused on the content!).</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">If You Translate from Slides in English (!!!), at Least Translate Well!</h3>

<p>For example, take care with the <em>adjective-noun</em> order in English, which becomes <em>noun-adjective</em> in Italian. So, <em>an efficient server</em> becomes <em>un server efficiente</em> and not <em>un efficiente server</em>, unless you are tempted to become a refined speaker and technological poet. Ah… but weren't you supposed to make the slides yourselves, for your own seminar? 😉</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Don't Write Too Much Text on the Slides!</h3>

<p>It is much more effective to write a bulleted list of things to say, perhaps indented by level, rather than writing out exactly all the text you intend to say. It takes a little more preparation (the things to say must not just be "read" but somehow remembered/improvised) but the result is more effective: in practice your talk is summarized (while you are delivering it) by the slide, which lets the listeners follow more easily what you are saying.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Be Careful with Images!</h3>

<p>In seminars you often add screenshots of an application, or charts taken directly from Excel. Here are some simple tips:</p>

<ul>
  <li>For Excel charts, select the chart, copy it, and paste it directly onto the slide, resizing it if needed inside PowerPoint. This will ensure acceptable image quality at any resolution, since the chart itself is kept as a vector inside the slide.</li>
  <li>For bitmap images (for example, screenshots of applications), take the screenshot (for example with ALT+Print Screen). Once copied, you can paste it directly onto the slide and in this way you will keep the resolution you have, and the images will be handled by PowerPoint without losing detail.</li>
  <li>Beware of artifacts when you create a <a aria-label=" (opens in a new tab)" href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JPEG" rel="noreferrer noopener">JPEG</a> file! When you create a JPEG file in a graphics program, set the compression quality to the minimum.</li>
</ul>

<h2 class="wp-block-heading">At the Graduation Session!</h2>

<p>Okay! You made it… only the presentation is left! Here are some useful tips!</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Don't Name Your Presentation "thesis.ppt"</h3>

<p>Or <strong>presentation.ppt</strong> or <strong>computer-science-degree.ppt</strong> or similar! The reason is simple: typically all the PPT presentations are loaded onto a single laptop shortly before the session, and in that case you end up with 10-15 presentations that all have more or less the same name! You can see that this causes delays and confusion. Name it with your first and last name, so as to make it easier to export.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Don't Underestimate Compatibility</h3>

<p>Check that your presentation is also compatible with older versions of PowerPoint. Also, don't use strange or unusual fonts (they might not be available on the platform hosting your presentation). Perhaps prepare the same presentation in two formats. And even in PDF, as an extreme backup.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Limit Animations</h3>

<p>Because, given the time limit, clicking forward repeatedly slows down the presentation, even if only slightly, and it also makes it harder and slower to go back if you made a mistake (one click too many because of nerves!).</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Watch the Battery</h3>

<p>If you use a laptop, plug it into the power supply to prevent it from shutting down during a presentation.</p>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">Clean Up Your Desktop and Your Machine</h3>

<p>This will be projected at the graduation session in front of a large audience (including relatives and friends!)… so remove the clutter, and perhaps, if you get "non-genuine Windows software" warnings 🙂, use another laptop (or take advantage of the free student licences from Microsoft Campus).</p>
